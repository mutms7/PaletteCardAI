How it works

Two tiny helpers,
one color story.

I think of the AI like two kids sharing one craft table. One names what it sees. The other sorts the paint.

1

First, there are pixels

A photo is really a big grid of tiny color squares. Like a mosaic, each square means almost nothing alone.

2

Then, patterns show up

The first helper has practiced with flowers, hearts, rings, cakes, and balloons. It combines little clues, then makes its best guess.

3

Next, the paint gets sorted

The second helper looks at five colors and gives each one a job. One becomes paper, another becomes a bright detail, and the rest support them.

4

Finally, I arrange the card

The models don’t draw every line. They pass their answers to three handmade layouts, which place your unchanged photo in the middle.

So, is it really AI?

Yes. Both model files are part of this prototype and run when you make cards. However, the card layouts and editor tools are regular code. That split keeps the result understandable and gives you the last word.

Privacy

Your photo is yours.

I only need it long enough to make your cards.

What gets sent

When you press “Make my 3 covers,” the selected photo and your card words go to this app’s server. Nothing is sent while you’re only previewing it.

What I keep

The server makes temporary card files, returns the result, and removes those files. The editor stays in your browser until you leave or reload.

What I don’t do

I don’t use your upload to retrain the models. I don’t add it to the repository, and I don’t deliberately build a photo library from it.

One honest limit

This is a working prototype, not a private photo vault. I’d still avoid uploading anything sensitive, just like you would with any small web demo.
